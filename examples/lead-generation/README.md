A complete `/consensus` run, unedited. Open [`report.html`](report.html) —
download it and open it in a browser, it is one self-contained file.

## What it found

The eight best-supported positions, by number of independent channels:

| Channels | Position |
|---|---|
| 14 | Choose one narrow market, and one able and willing to spend |
| 14 | Define the ideal customer profile before prospecting, refine it from who actually buys |
| 13 | Trigger outreach off an observed signal rather than a static filter |
| 12 | Market the result the buyer gets, priced in money — never the service or mechanism |
| 12 | Only demonstrated results and named proof move a sceptical prospect |
| 12 | Cold calling is a volume-and-repetition skill with knowable dial-to-meeting ratios |
| 12 | Every message needs real per-prospect research; a merge field is not personalisation |
| 10 | Expensive leads are almost never the real problem — the offer and revenue per customer are |

**Four positions came out expired** — backed by 6 or 7 older channels and by
nobody in the last twelve months:

- Pick one lead channel and go all in
- Work your existing contacts first, and ask who they know
- Word of mouth is the most profitable source if you engineer it
- The first message should aim only for a reply, not a booked meeting

The first is the interesting one, because its opposite — *run several channels
at once and stack them on the same prospect* — is settled with 11 channels
behind it. The tool recorded those two as contradicting each other, then found
one alive and one dead. That is a real shift in the field rather than noise.

**69 contested pairs.** The field is genuinely split on short versus long cold
emails, whether subject lines matter at all, following a call script word for
word versus working from a framework, cash up front versus delivering first,
and inbound versus outbound.

## What this example also shows

The first pass of this run reported **ten** expired positions, including
"follow-ups produce most booked meetings" — which is bedrock sales advice, not
dead advice. That was a sampling bug, not a finding: the recent half of the
corpus was 49% drawn from a date-filtered search that reaches newer and more
tactical videos, while the older half could only come from relevance ranking,
which favours evergreen strategy videos. Compared directly, ordinary advice
looked dead.

The absence test now runs only over videos relevance search reached — the one
selection mechanism that spans both periods — and a single recent backer
anywhere blocks an expired verdict outright. That took expired from ten to
four and raised the minimum backing from three channels to six.

The report says which sample the verdicts were tested on, and why it is
narrower than the corpus. Read the banner at the top before the findings.

## Caveat worth keeping

Those four expired verdicts sit at p = 0.044 to 0.069, close to the 0.10
threshold, against 25 comparable recent videos. Treat them as suggestive. The
settled positions, with 10 to 14 independent channels each, are the solid part.

This measures what creators **say**, not what is true.
